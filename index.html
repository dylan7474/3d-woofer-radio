3D Woofer Radio

A compact, battery-powered radio with a woofer in the centre, a mid-range speaker either side and a tweeter at each end.

Idea stage. Nothing is built or tested yet. The numbers here are first-pass estimates from published speaker data.

tweetermidwoofermidtweeter

The idea

Inspired by the look and layout of the 1980s "3D Super Woofer" boomboxes, but not a clone: our own size and parts, with no tape decks or CD. A wide, low, rectangular cabinet with a black metal speaker grille across the front, each speaker sitting in its own pod (a recessed section of the grille, with its own sealed chamber behind), and a built-in rechargeable battery. A "luxury" build that uses parts a mass producer would probably skip.

Speaker pods: first-pass sums

  • Mid pods: with the woofer taking over at about 130–150 Hz, a sealed pod of about 2–3 L per FR 10 is enough (roll-off roughly 118–133 Hz).
  • Woofer pod: about 4 L sealed (roll-off near 100 Hz) or about 2.2 L (near 130 Hz).
  • Width: roughly 500–550 mm for all five speakers side by side.

These come from the standard sealed-box formula and published speaker parameters. Real enclosures and drivers will differ, so the plan is to build and measure.

Things to check

  • Not every "2.1 TPA3116" board has a working crossover: buy one whose listing names the low-pass control.
  • The sub output is rated 100 W into 2 Ω; a 4 Ω woofer gets less, and 12 V gives less again, though still plenty for a 5″ woofer in a small box.
  • A 4 Ω tweeter in parallel with a 4 Ω mid would look like about 2 Ω, so use 8 Ω tweeters.
  • Battery safety, amp ventilation and cabinet weight.

Next steps
